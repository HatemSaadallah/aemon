## Outcome

- **recommendation:** Invest in a differentiated consumer proposition that positions the membrane as a premium, health‑enhancing solution addressing taste, safety, and convenience concerns. Begin with a limited pilot in a niche segment (e.g., health‑conscious households) to test demand and willingness to pay before broader rollout.
- **target customer:** Health‑conscious consumers who currently rely on bottled water or prefer a premium water experience, particularly in markets where tap water quality is perceived as uncertain.
- **value proposition:** A differentiated membrane offering superior taste, safety, and health benefits, supported by easy installation and low maintenance, positioned as a premium home water purifier that eliminates the need for bottled water.
- **favoured theory:** T1
- **next action:** Run the proposed Membrane Positioning Choice Test (500 Danish consumers, 8 weeks, €12,000) to measure willingness to pay for premium positioning versus convenience positioning. Use the decision rule to decide whether to proceed with premium
- **uncertainty:** The size of the premium‑positioning demand and the willingness to pay for health benefits remain uncertain. Evidence from the test will clarify whether consumers are willing to pay at least 20% more for a premium membrane.
- **would change if:** ["If the test shows convenience positioning yields at least 20% higher willingness to pay and reduced hassle is the primary reason, we would shift focus to a convenience‑focused proposition.", "If the test fails to show a premium advantage or if willingness to pay is below the 20% threshold, we would recommend no expansion and continue focusing on current B2B membranes."]
