## Outcome

- **recommendation:** Invest in a differentiated consumer proposition that positions the membrane as a premium, health‑enhancing solution addressing taste, safety, and convenience concerns. Begin with a limited pilot in a niche segment (e.g., health‑conscious households) to test demand and willingness to pay before broader rollout.
- **target customer:** Health‑conscious consumers who currently rely on bottled water or prefer a premium water experience, particularly in Denmark and potentially other markets where tap water quality is perceived as uncertain.
- **value proposition:** A high‑performance membrane that eliminates taste concerns, provides clear health benefits, and offers easy installation and low maintenance, enabling premium pricing and subscription services for maintenance.
- **favoured theory:** T1
- **next action:** Run the proposed messaging test (500 participants, 8 weeks, €15,000) to compare premium vs. convenience messaging and determine whether premium positioning drives higher purchase intent and willingness to pay.
- **uncertainty:** The size of the premium‑willing segment, actual willingness to pay, and the ability to scale production and distribution remain uncertain. Evidence of strong demand for a differentiated proposition is needed before committing resources.
- **would change if:** ["If the messaging test shows convenience messaging achieving at least 15 percentage points higher purchase intent and a €10 price premium, or if a survey reveals that taste/safety is the primary barrier to tap water use, we would shift focus", "to a convenience‑focused proposition and explore low‑upfront‑cost models."]
