# Candidate manufacturers and the strategic question

Prepared November 1, 2026 from public sources only. **This is external analysis.** None of these companies has been contacted or has asked this question, and nothing here describes their intentions.

Record IDs refer to `strategy/dossier/evidence.json`, where each one has its URL, date and exact excerpt. We fetched every excerpt again from its live source: 136 of 142 matched and are in the dossier, and the other 6 were left out. Most company facts below are the company's own claims.

## Recommendation

**Moldex-Metric, provisionally**, with Cotral Lab and Hellberg Safety as comparison cases.

**The unresolved question:**

> Should Moldex develop a differentiated consumer proposition for a specific use case, beyond its existing hearing-protection offering? What evidence would justify that investment?

Product redesign, venue distribution, a new customer group and no expansion all stay open as real alternatives.

**Correction:** an earlier version said we had found no Moldex consumer line, and framed consumers as an entirely new market. That was too strong. Moldex's own page lists small blister packs and a display of blisters (`mdx-spark-blister`, `mdx-spark-display`), a reseller calls one a retail pack (`mdx-retail-listing`), and affiliate review sites recommend Moldex plugs for sleep (`mdx-sleep-review-1`, `mdx-sleep-review-2`). This does not show a consumer strategy, and we do not know how many individuals buy them. It does mean some individuals can already buy these products. The agent's briefs no longer state this premise, so it has to find out.

**Why this question is open and worth asking:**

- **Each theory implies a different business move.** Appearance points to a new product and brand. Availability points to selling to venues, which is closer to how Moldex already sells. Sleep points to a different customer and possibly a lighter regulatory route. Comfort may turn out to be a belief more than a product flaw.
- **The evidence does not settle it.** The best causal study found that use rose in a cluster-randomised trial of messages, on-site sales and an ear check bundled together, so availability cannot be separated from prompting (`crutzen-2021-celebrate-safe`). We found no study comparing appearance, comfort and availability on real behaviour. Exact quotes prove what a source says, not what it establishes: see the `does_not_establish` annotations in the dossier.
- **Hindsight does not answer it.** Loop shows that a consumer brand can work. It does not show that an industrial foam-plug maker should copy it, and its own move into everyday use reportedly did not last (`cnbc-2023-loop-pivot`).

## Why Moldex

1. **It is an open case.** Hearing protection is stated core expertise, and we found small packs but no sign of a dedicated consumer proposition.
2. **Its capability fits more than one theory.** In-house moulding and foam could serve venue supply or sleep, not only a redesigned product. Production volumes are not public.
3. **It has the most useful parallel to the Del Vecchio example.** The same capability has already been reused in a different market once.

The main cost is that we know little about its finances, so the agent has to treat economics as an open assumption and say so. That is more honest than guessing, and an investigation that says what it cannot know is part of what we want it to learn.

## What the evidence says so far

These are analyst readings to be tested, not findings. No study here compares the theories head to head.

| Theory | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Appearance** | Looks mattered in a listening study (`bockstael-2015-looks`), and a quarter of surveyed Gen Z adults reported embarrassment (`rnid-2026-embarrassed`) | Not among the top reasons in the abstracts we could read (`long-2024-nonusers`, `gilles-2014-reasons`) |
| **Comfort** | A stated reason for low use (`gilles-2014-reasons`, `rnid-2026-reasons`) | Most did not agree that plugs are uncomfortable (`psych-review-2025-comfort`), and users rated them better than never-users expected (`loughran-2021-comfort`) |
| **Availability** | Strongest causal evidence, but bundled (`crutzen-2021-celebrate-safe`). A smaller study suggests providing plugs mattered, though both of its arms received them (`beach-2016-provision`) | Swiss promoters must hand out plugs, yet many never wear them, in a company survey with no comparison group (`sonova-2019-swiss-free-plugs`) |
| **Other customers** | Sleep lab evidence (`basner-2026-sleep`), and EU guidelines list plugs not designed to protect against hazards, such as sleep plugs, as not PPE (`eu-guidelines-sleep-not-ppe`) | Demand is unproven, and Loop's everyday pivot reportedly faded (`cnbc-2023-loop-pivot`) |

Recurring explanations outside these four, such as never having thought about it (`long-2024-nonusers`), may matter more than any of them. A good investigation should notice that.
