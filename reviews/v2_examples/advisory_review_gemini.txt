### Overall Observations
1. **Monoculture of method:** All 17 proposals default to online surveys measuring stated preference or hypothetical WTP; none measure real commitment (pre-orders, deposits, trial usage) or conduct physical field/retail experiments.
2. **Fatal historical anachronisms:** Four historical cases (Nalgene 1970, Maglite 1983, OXO 1989, Diatek 1991) propose online survey panels decades before the commercial internet existed.
3. **Disproportionate decision rules:** Almost all rules treat a minor stated-preference gap in a cheap (€3k–€20k) survey as justification to commit to full-scale commercial manufacturing, rather than triggering a bounded de-risking stage (e.g., prototype, pilot launch, or partner test).
4. **Persuasion over mechanism:** Tests consistently vary promotional copy or ad framing rather than underlying product trade-offs, testing what message sounds appealing rather than why consumers will actually buy and retain a physical product.
5. **Logic inversions and confounding:** Several designs embed critical methodological flaws—such as FLIR (#3) abandoning expansion if a product demo succeeds, Embrace (#7) confounding price points and positioning across disparate countries, and ARRI (#11) accepting the null hypothesis as proof for T1.
