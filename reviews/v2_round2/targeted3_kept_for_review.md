## 1. andis-new-evidence-irrelevant (present day, from the test step)

**Question:** Should Andis develop a further differentiated proposition for people who cut hair at home, beyond its existing at-home range, or concentrate on professional barbers and groomers? What evidence would justify either choice?

- **T1:** Home users will adopt a differentiated at-home proposition if they perceive it as a premium, professional-quality experience that builds confidence and reduces salon visits.
- **T2:** Home users will adopt a differentiated at-home proposition if they perceive it as a convenient, time-saving solution that reduces the need for salon visits.

**Sources read (dossier date):** andis-1997-home-kits (unknown (International Directory of Company Histories, Volume 85)), andis-athome-prices (unknown (accessed 2026-11-03)), andis-attest-20-dollars (2022-05-12), andis-diyers (unknown (press-release boilerplate, 2023 or 2024)), andis-home-line (unknown (accessed 2026-11-03)), andis-trusted-home-users (2025-02-28), andis-wahl-10-percent (2021-11-08), andis-wahl-6-million (2021-03-15)

**Test:** Premium vs. Convenience Kit Choice Test. Online choice experiment with refundable deposit to simulate real purchase commitment

**Sample:** 300 U.S. adults who have cut hair at home in the past 6 months, recruited via an online panel (estimated cost €4,500 for panel access and incentives)  
**Measure:** 1) Choice: participants select which kit they would buy (Premium or Convenience). 2) Deposit: participants pay a refundable €5 deposit to secure a pre‑order for a limited run of the chosen kit. 3) Willingness to Pay (WTP): participants state maximum price they would pay for the chosen kit. 4) Follow‑up survey after 4 weeks to confirm intent to keep the deposit.  
**Cost:** €20000, 12 weeks; theories tested: T1, T2

- **Predicts T1:** Premium kit will be chosen by >55% of participants; average WTP for Premium will be at least 20% higher than for Convenience; deposit conversion to final purchase will be >70%.
- **Predicts T2:** Convenience kit will be chosen by >55% of participants; average WTP for Convenience will be at least 15% higher than for Premium; deposit conversion will be >70%.

**Decision rule:** If the proportion choosing Premium >55% AND average WTP Premium > WTP Convenience + 10%, recommend developing a further differentiated premium at‑home proposition. If the proportion choosing Convenience >55% AND average WTP Convenience > WTP Premium + 10%, recommend a convenience‑focused differentiated proposition. Otherwise, maintain current mix and focus on professional segment. The test budget of €20,000 covers panel recruitment, deposit processing, kit mock‑ups, incentives, and analysis. The 12‑week timeline allows recruitment (2 weeks), experiment (4 weeks), follow‑up (4 weeks), and reporting (2 weeks).

**Teacher, learned:** We have identified Andis’ existing at‑home line and evidence of a surge in home haircutting (6 million new users, 42% market growth). However, no direct evidence shows whether home users value professional quality or convenience enough to pay a premium. Two open theories explain adoption: T1 (premium professional experience) and T2 (convenient time‑saving). A test that measures real purchase commitment to differentiated propositions can distinguish these mechanisms.

**Teacher, uncertain:** Which driver – perceived professional quality or perceived convenience – is more compelling for home users, and whether they will pay a premium for either.

**Teacher, why next:** A low‑cost experiment offering two differentiated kits (premium‑branded vs. convenience‑branded) and measuring actual purchase decisions (deposit or pre‑order) will reveal which proposition drives higher willingness to pay and purchase intent, directly testing the core assumptions of T1 and T2.

## 2. cotral-new-evidence-irrelevant (present day, from the test step)

**Question:** Should Cotral Lab develop a differentiated consumer proposition for a specific use case, beyond its existing hearing-protection offering? What evidence would justify that investment?

- **T1:** Professional musicians will adopt custom-moulded hearing protection if it is marketed as a performanceenhancing tool that preserves sound quality while blocking noise, because they value accurate sound and are willing to pay a premium for a product that does not compromise their craft.
- **T2:** Sleep aid users will adopt custom-moulded hearing protection if it is marketed as a medical-grade solution that reduces sleep disruption caused by background noise, because they seek a product that addresses a specific health concern and are willing to pay for a solution that improves sleep quality.

**Sources read (dossier date):** bockstael-2015-looks (2015-07 to 2015-08), ctl-3d (unknown), ctl-channel (2025 (exact date not shown)), ctl-deal (2025 (exact date not shown)), ctl-filter (2024-04-02 (last updated)), ctl-it1 (unknown), ctl-onsite (unknown), minuendo-claim (unknown (accessed 2026-11-01))

**Test:** Segment‑Framed Pre‑Order Test. Online recruitment of two target segments (musicians and sleep‑aid users). Randomly assign each participant to receive either a musician‑framed offer (performance‑enhancing, sound‑preserving) or a sleep‑framed offer (medical‑grade, sleep‑disruption reduction). Offer a refundable €50 deposit for a custom‑moulded earplug priced at €150, with delivery promised within 8 weeks. Measure deposit acceptance rate by segment and framing.

**Sample:** Recruit 200 musicians (via music schools, forums, local orchestras) and 200 sleep‑aid users (via insomnia support groups, sleep‑clinic newsletters). Randomly split each group into two equal halves (100 each) for musician‑framed vs. sleep‑framed offers. Total participants = 400.  
**Measure:** Primary outcome: proportion of participants who pay the €50 deposit within 7 days of offer. Secondary: self‑reported willingness to pay €150 for the product. Compare deposit rates: T1 predicts higher deposit rate among musicians receiving musician‑framed offer than among musicians receiving sleep‑framed offer. T2 predicts higher deposit rate among sleep‑aid users receiving sleep‑framed offer than among those receiving musician‑framed offer.  
**Cost:** €20000, 12 weeks; theories tested: T1, T2

- **Predicts T1:** Musicians offered musician‑framed earplugs will have deposit rate ≥ 30%, significantly higher than musicians offered sleep‑framed earplugs (expected ≈ 10%).
- **Predicts T2:** Sleep‑aid users offered sleep‑framed earplugs will have deposit rate ≥ 30%, significantly higher than sleep‑aid users offered musician‑framed earplugs (expected ≈ 10%).

**Decision rule:** If deposit rate for musician‑framed musicians ≥ 30% AND deposit rate for sleep‑framed sleep‑aid users ≥ 30%, proceed to develop differentiated propositions for both segments. If only one condition meets the threshold, develop that segment‑specific proposition and test the other later. If neither meets 30%, decline investment in differentiated consumer proposition and focus on existing B2B channel.

**Teacher, learned:** Cotral Lab currently sells custom-moulded hearing protection primarily through B2B channels. Public sources show it offers Bluetooth-enabled models and frequency-specific filters, but no evidence directly links these to consumer demand for musicians or sleep aid users. A 2015 study of musicians highlighted that comfort and looks drive appreciation more than sound quality, suggesting that a performance‑enhancing claim may need to emphasize non‑auditory benefits. No independent data exist on willingness to pay for a differentiated consumer proposition.

**Teacher, uncertain:** Two theories compete: T1 argues musicians will pay a premium for sound‑preserving protection marketed as performance‑enhancing; T2 argues sleep‑aid users will pay for a medical‑grade solution that reduces sleep disruption. Both lack direct evidence of purchase intent. The test must determine which framing generates real commitment from the target segment.

**Teacher, why next:** A low‑cost, 12‑week pilot can test real purchase intent by offering a refundable deposit for a limited run of custom‑moulded earplugs with segment‑specific marketing. This will distinguish the theories because T1 predicts higher deposit uptake when the product is framed for musicians, while T2 predicts the opposite for sleep‑aid framing. The result will guide whether to invest in a differentiated consumer proposition or stick with existing B2B focus.

**Flags:** asks for a real commitment (deposit, pre-order, paid use); the arms differ in framing or message
